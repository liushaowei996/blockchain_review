\section{Review Scope and Analytical Lens}\label{sec:scope}

This narrative review uses a problem-driven synthesis to integrate mission-trust concepts. Its principal discovery window covers English-language publications from 2016 to 6 August 2026, supplemented by earlier foundational work on reputation, distributed-system limits, provenance, and consensus. Peer-reviewed studies and reviews, scholarly books, standards, and official technical references supply complementary evidence. Discovery combined 32 topical OpenAlex queries, seed reviews, and targeted reference tracing across six themes: UAV blockchain and swarm security; maritime and underwater communication and security; heterogeneous UAV--USV--UUV cooperation; IoT trust and reputation; provenance, attestation, and zero trust; and permissioned-ledger architecture and governance. The discovery script retrieved up to 25 relevance-ranked results per query and deduplicated by DOI or OpenAlex identifier. This bounded retrieval defines the conceptual sample used in the synthesis.

Sources were retained for their relevance to an OAL assurance claim, an operational constraint, an implementation assumption, or an evaluation resource. Transferable mechanisms from adjacent domains were retained with their source context identified. This purposive selection supports source-specific conceptual comparisons. Systematic estimates of mechanism prevalence would require a defined sampling frame, complete exclusion tracking, and assessment of screening agreement. Supplementary Note S3 records the query set, evidence-status definitions, and coding locators available for the detailed comparisons.

The analytical unit is a \emph{claim--object--context} tuple: which object and property are judged, at what mission stage, under which network, threat, and governance assumptions, and with what evidence? Table S1 records bibliographic identity and these analytical fields; Table S2 records acquisition separately from coding. The inventory contains 141 cited sources. Reconciliation on 7 September 2026 identified retained full-text artifacts for 73 sources, including 9 official references. The 33-source acquisition batch completed in August is a subset of those artifacts. Sixteen sources have detailed coding for the review's selected method and resource comparisons; a foundational consensus-limit claim was additionally checked against primary text during this revision. Availability and analytical coverage are recorded separately. Title-derived fields serve as routing categories; assessments of source coverage and capabilities rely on claim-level inspection. The September revision used the August discovery export as its retrieval baseline and checked selected claims and reference status.

The synthesis compares source-supported mechanisms and their conditions of use. For example, a consensus mechanism that is feasible on shore servers may be unsuitable on a battery-limited UUV, and a threshold acceptable for routine monitoring may be unsafe for emergency intervention. Detailed comparisons use the coded source's configuration and demonstrated scope; broad thematic descriptions identify representative sources. The synthesis brings together OAL, the dependency grammar, CTG loop, evidence-maturity profile, and CDUTP as conceptual relationships and design requirements for empirical and formal evaluation.

The evidence base includes both general and domain-specific sources. General IoT and blockchain literature supplies reusable mechanisms, while cross-domain unmanned-system studies supply operational constraints. Cross-domain transfer is treated as an engineering hypothesis and evaluated against the target environment's delay, connectivity, resource, threat, and consequence conditions. For example, a road-vehicle trust model can inform context-aware evidence aggregation when its assumptions are re-evaluated under acoustic delay; an industrial permissioned ledger can inform policy consistency when its reconciliation behavior is tested for disconnected underwater operation.

Table~\ref{tab:scope} summarizes the analytical focus and its complementary context. Satellite, high-altitude, shore, cloud, and edge nodes serve as supporting infrastructure for the three vehicle domains. The mission lifecycle extends through audit, revocation, and recovery because trustworthy operations require action after anomalies: credentials expire, compromised devices are quarantined, policies roll back, partitions reconcile, and evidence remains available for accountability. This endpoint locates blockchain's strongest plausible value in shared state and traceable decisions across organizations, with hard real-time physical control assigned to local safety mechanisms.
