\section{Review Scope and Methods}\label{sec:scope}

\subsection{Identification and Selection}

This is a structured narrative review. The original bibliography contained 141 references spanning unmanned cooperation, communication and security, trust inference, provenance, attestation, and distributed governance. The retained discovery script queried OpenAlex on 6 August 2026 using 32 English search strings, a publication-date filter starting on 1 January 2016, and the 25 highest-ranked results per query. Deduplication by DOI or OpenAlex identifier produced 571 candidate records. Queries included ``blockchain UAV swarm'', ``underwater wireless sensor network trust management'', ``UAV USV UUV cooperative systems'', ``remote attestation IoT survey RATS'', and ``blockchain network partition finality''. The complete query list and retrieval settings accompany the supplementary materials. Citation following and targeted retrieval added earlier foundations and official specifications. Crossref metadata supported bibliographic checking; it was not a second independent screening database.

Selection favored English sources that described cross-domain operating constraints, specified a security or trust mechanism applicable to a mission boundary, or supplied a relevant standard, dataset, or simulator. Duplicate records and sources without a substantive connection to those questions were excluded from the synthesis. Reviews supplied context and citation leads; original mechanism studies supplied the analytical sample. Foundational work predating 2016 was retained for reputation, distributed consistency, provenance, and feedback architectures. Targeted publisher, author-repository and official-source retrieval during revision checked developments through 25 October 2026.

The expansion revisited all 14 original mechanism candidates outside the initial coding, adding four after inspecting their methods and evaluations. It also added eight domain studies on AIS protection, maritime monitoring, UAV trust/routing, and underwater mechanisms, together with four already cited consensus comparators. Ten original candidates remained unavailable or only partly inspectable; access status is recorded separately from scientific eligibility. The supplementary expansion register documents these 26 candidates and four contextual leads, with source versions and successful retrieval URLs. The historical workflow did not retain all title/abstract rejection decisions or search-engine rankings. The 571 discovery records, 141 original citations, targeted expansion checks and final bibliography therefore describe different stages of a narrative synthesis, rather than a reconstructed systematic-review flow.

\subsection{Full-Text Coding and Reproducible Synthesis}\label{sec:coding}

The analytical sample comprises 39 original mechanism contributions with inspected full texts. Its direct-domain stratum contains 11 UAV, three maritime AIS, and five intended underwater studies. Twenty transferable studies address reputation, fog offloading, provenance, attestation, access control, shared state, or observation appraisal. Application domain and actual evaluation setting are recorded separately: a terrestrial-data experiment proposed for underwater use remains a proxy evaluation. The four generic consensus comparators form an identifiable, removable subset. These purposively selected mechanisms support comparison across assurance boundaries; selection does not estimate prevalence in the field.

The revised bibliography contains 123 references. The 39 coded studies are a subset; the remaining 84 support operational context, conceptual foundations, standards, related synthesis, or evaluation resources. A reference-role register makes this distinction explicit. Source identifiers S01--S39, claims, supporting passages and evaluation scope appear in \texttt{oal-study-mapping.csv}; the study register adds source versions, domain strata and retrieval links. Full-text PDFs are not redistributed.

The coding unit is an explicitly described mechanism claim. Each of the 89 records associates one object with one assurance layer and the supported lifecycle stages. Several claims can be coded for one paper; unrelated labels are never expanded into an automatic Cartesian product. A heatmap cell counts each study once for that exact object--layer--stage combination. Counts describe coverage, not additive sample sizes or effect sizes. Layer co-occurrence within a paper identifies a possible interface; validated transfer between claims requires its own evidence.

Evidence profiles record conceptual argument (L0), analytical/security reasoning (L1), simulation or recorded-data evaluation (L2), implemented prototype/testbed (L3), physical operating trials (L4), and sustained multi-party operation (L5). These categories are complementary evidence forms, not an ordinal quality score. A profile is attached to the stated mechanism and documented evaluation scope; an implementation benchmark does not validate every assurance claim made in its surrounding architecture. Recorded field data analyzed offline are coded L2; a hardware prototype without described operating conditions is coded L3. Ambiguous deployment claims are assigned the narrower evidenced category and explained in the extraction note.

Coding used one extraction process with passage checks; independent duplicate coding and inter-rater agreement were not performed. A decision log documents ambiguous object assignments, prototype scope and application/data mismatches. Reproducible sensitivity analyses separate the two strata, remove conceptual-only studies or the four generic consensus comparators, exclude the underwater proxy experiment, vary identity and physical-trial coding, and omit each study in turn. These analyses test map stability under stated choices. Empty cells denote no coded claim; mission-specific applicability and global research absence cannot be inferred from a zero.
