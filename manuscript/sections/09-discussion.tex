\section{Discussion and Research Agenda}

\subsection{Integration, Governance, and Privacy}

OAL makes the composition problem explicit: provenance concerns history, observation validity concerns the world, shared-state consistency concerns agreement, and mission suitability concerns consequence. A policy specifies how these claims combine for a given use. Dependency analysis identifies where a changed key, model, input, or reference value invalidates an assumption. It also exposes architectural common causes: adding several UUVs does not remove their dependence on one gateway, clock, or operator.

Consortium design must specify admission, contract/model approval, emergency authority, disputes, member exit, and audit responsibility. Maritime adoption research and the TradeLens experience show why participation and incentives belong in this analysis \citep{zhou2020key,farah2024survey,maersk2022tradelens}. The endorsement rule in the example preserves joint control while creating a measurable availability cost through its mandatory signers. Additional replicas under one owner do not create additional independent governance domains.

Cross-sector adoption studies also distinguish the claimed benefits of traceability from the organizational changes needed to realize them \citep{kshetri20181,lnes2017blockchain}. For a coalition, the unit of accountability is the participating authority and its commitments, not simply the number of ledger nodes. A useful deployment agreement assigns evidence retention, disclosure permissions, service maintenance, and incident-response duties before a mission. It defines what remains available when an organization leaves and which signatures continue to support historical judgments after key rotation. These provisions can be implemented with a validating log or ledger; they form part of the matched governance assumptions in a fair comparison.

Privacy depends on more than payload encryption. Stable identifiers, route custody, endorsement timing, and model-use records can reveal vehicle activity and organizational relationships \citep{reyna2018blockchain,kshetri2017can}. Data minimization, selective disclosure, retention, and controlled identity resolution therefore accompany auditability. A revocation invalidates future reliance under a policy; it does not erase a retained commitment. Human override is a separately authorized, recorded transition with its evidence, rationale, and consequence.

\subsection{Falsifiable Research Propositions}\label{sec:propositions}

The following propositions define future comparative tests. Each experiment prespecifies its mission distribution, minimum useful effect, primary outcome, and analysis rule. Null and adverse results identify conditions where the additional structure provides no benefit.

\textbf{P1 (claim organization).} Test whether a separately represented assurance vector reduces held-out unsafe acceptance by a prespecified amount at matched false-isolation and latency budgets, relative to a tuned scalar policy receiving the same complete evidence. The scalar comparator includes non-compensable authorization/freshness gates and may encode interactions. Both use the same actions and equal tuning budgets. The proposition fails if the matched comparator performs equally well or better; the illustrative veto-aware scalar in Table~\ref{tab:trace} is one such equality case. Representation alone does not guarantee improvement.

\textbf{P2 (dependency modeling).} Under prespecified shared-source error and collusion rates, test whether a model with common-cause variables improves a proper scoring rule and mission loss relative to an equally tuned independence model. Include correct, incomplete, and erroneous lineage conditions. The proposition fails when model misspecification or acquisition cost offsets the gain, or when observations are sufficiently independent that no useful improvement remains.

\textbf{P3 (diagnosis and unknown status).} Compare a model retaining benign, adversarial, and unresolved hypotheses with a binary appraisal model under mixed degradation, attacks, and evidence outages. Both can choose the same actions, including abstention. Separate diagnosis and abstention ablations, and measure loss from unsafe acceptance, false isolation, corroboration delay, and lost availability. The proposition fails if the added diagnostic state increases loss or yields no useful improvement.

\textbf{P4 (offline authority).} Vary delegation scope, expiry, clock uncertainty, partition duration, and revocation time. Test whether bounded delegation offers a useful trade-off between tasks completed before deadline and actions conflicting with effective global policy, compared with fresh-global-only authorization and unrestricted offline autonomy. Report locally authorized actions and retrospectively conflicting actions separately. Failure includes operating regimes where the bounded policy is dominated on both outcomes.

\textbf{P5 (conditional ledger value).} Compare an implemented permissioned ledger with a policy-validating independently witnessed log under equal membership, fault domains, validation rules, retention, and workload. Vary $n$, responsive quorum, partition duration $D$, event rate, and recovery backlog. For deadline $\tau$, estimate the boundary $D+L(n,\lambda)\leq\tau$, where $L$ is measured post-contact completion delay at load $\lambda$. Test for a prespecified improvement in timely, mutually verifiable decisions or recovery completeness at a fixed byte/energy budget. Equal safety with higher ledger cost, lower availability from mandatory endorsers, or no improvement over the validating log refutes the proposed advantage in that regime. The 58/59 s boundary in Section~\ref{sec:budget} illustrates this test with assumed delays; it is not its empirical result.

\textbf{P6 (evaluation coverage).} Select configurations from identical candidate sets and equal evaluation budgets using either transaction/network metrics alone or those metrics plus observation, endpoint, governance, and mission outcomes. Compare held-out mission loss under fixed selection rules. The proposition fails if the broader evaluation does not improve selection after its additional acquisition and interpretation costs are included.

\subsection{Assurance Cases and Maritime Requirements}\label{sec:assurance-case}

OAL supplies the content of an assurance argument; existing notations supply its structure. GSN represents claims, strategies, context, assumptions, and evidence, while SACM provides a structured assurance-case metamodel \citep{gsn2021,sacm2023}. UL 4600 supplies safety-case requirements for autonomous products rather than a diagram notation \citep{ul4600}. OAL can annotate their claims with object/version, assurance layer, lifecycle stage, event interval, and evidence profile. It does not replace those standards or establish compliance with them.

For a claim that a fused product is suitable for route replanning, subclaims identify credible observations, protected derivation, valid authority, current policy, and acceptable uncertainty. A failed dependency is a defeater or evidence gap requiring review. The argument retains observation, issue, receipt, and expiry times, and identifies decisions made under cached authority. A corrected input or revoked key triggers review of affected descendants while preserving independently supported products. Validation should measure critical evidence gaps, seeded invalid arguments, reviewer error, and time to identify affected products.

Maritime requirements add a specific application boundary. IMO's interim guidelines for MASS trials and regulatory scoping exercise provide trial and regulatory context \citep{imo2019trials,imo2021scoping}. As of the revision date, the non-mandatory MASS Code had been adopted in May 2026 and taken effect on 1 July 2026 \citep{imo2026mass}. DNV-CG-0264 addresses autonomous and remotely operated ships \citep{dnv2024autonomous}. An assurance case must identify the vessel and operation to which these instruments apply, the responsible authority, operational design conditions, human/remote-control responsibilities, and evidence of safe fallback. Their maritime scope cannot be transferred wholesale to UAV or underwater operations. A ledger record can trace an approval or operating limit; the substantive safety evidence and applicable approval process remain separate.

\subsection{Toward a Cross-Domain Unmanned Trust Profile}

The proposed \CDUTP{} is an interoperability agenda for versioned claims and evidence references. It can reuse RATS roles, W3C PROV relations, zero-trust policy concepts, and secure-update manifests \citep{birkholz2023remote,w3c2013provo,rose2020zero,ietf2020suit}. UAS and maritime service specifications supply communications context \citep{threegpp22125,threegpp22119}. A profile instance records object/type, scoped claim, issuer and relying role, evidence reference, event and receipt times, expiry, uncertainty, permitted use, policy/model/schema versions, disclosure conditions, dependencies, and revocation binding.

The compact encoding in Section~\ref{sec:pattern} is one illustrative transport profile. Interoperability requires version-specific semantic mappings and tests, including bounded clocks, key rotation, missing evidence, and disconnected issuance. Independent implementations can then be compared in plugfests and multi-organization exercises. The key research question is whether common semantics reduce decision and audit errors at an acceptable communication and governance cost.
