\section{Operational Context and Trust Foundations}

\subsection{A Heterogeneous Mission System of Systems}\label{sec:coalition}

UAVs contribute rapid repositioning, line-of-sight radio relays, and electro-optical sensing. USVs carry larger energy stores and antennas and bridge acoustic and radio networks. UUVs operate close to underwater targets but often rely on low-rate acoustic links or delayed surfacing for large transfers. Communication surveys describe the resulting trade-offs among range, bandwidth, latency, energy, and alignment \citep{nomikos2023survey,ali2020recent,saeed2019underwater,jahanbakht2021internet}. Topology, clock quality, and reachability can change within a mission.

Consider a civil coastal-response coalition comprising authority A, research operator B, communications provider C, and contracted data provider D. B operates a UUV and UAV; C supplies the USV relay; A approves mission authority; D contributes independent data and audit services. Each organization retains its administrative identity. A local operator can authenticate a device while lacking authority to change the coalition's membership, evidence policy, or revocation history. The case in Sections~\ref{sec:trace} and~\ref{sec:pattern} uses this coalition to specify which changes require joint approval.

A USV may simultaneously relay acoustic traffic, cache data, provide time, and host fusion. Failure at that shared dependency affects several apparently independent products. End-to-end source signatures can preserve origin integrity through an untrusted relay, while selective withholding and common clock or transformation errors remain relevant. Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} separates the constrained evidence path from shore-based shared-state services.

Raw imagery, sonar, detailed telemetry, and attestation evidence remain encrypted off chain. Shared records contain commitments, membership, policy/model versions, revocations, and audit judgments. Delay-tolerant forwarding can use Bundle Protocol version 7 and BPSec integrity/confidentiality services \citep{rfc9171,rfc9172}. Store-and-forward delivery preserves messages across contacts; authority expiry and evidence-age policy are separate application rules. A successfully delivered old command is not thereby timely.

\subsection{Mission Trust and the Relying Decision}

\emph{Mission trust} is a context-specific decision to rely on an object for a stated purpose, given available evidence and the consequences of error. \emph{Trustworthiness} denotes the supporting properties: identity, measured endpoint state, link availability, provenance, freshness, observation quality, and accountable governance. The same image may support broad situational awareness while being unsuitable for collision avoidance.

For object $o$, time $t$, context $c$, and mission use $m$, appraisal and decision have distinct interfaces:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
T_o(t,c,m)&=\mathcal{A}(o,\mathcal{E}_{\leq t},c,m;\theta),\\
D_{r,o}(t,c,m)&=\pi_r\!\left(T_o(t,c,m),\mathcal{C}_t,m\right).
\end{aligned}\label{eq:trust}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal{E}_{\leq t}$ contains evidence received by decision time, including observation times and lineage. The appraisal $\mathcal{A}$ produces claim-specific outcomes and uncertainty under assumptions $\theta$. Relying party $r$ applies policy $\pi_r$ under authority, deadline, consequence, and resource constraints $\mathcal{C}_t$. The result may permit, restrict, defer, or reject use. Subjective opinions, intervals, ordinal states, and reputation values can implement this interface \citep{jsang2016subjective,mui2003computational,kamvar2003eigentrust}. A probabilistic interpretation requires a defined event and calibration evidence.

A \emph{trusted data environment} is the socio-technical setting that produces, transforms, shares, and governs this evidence. It contains principals, devices, paths, artifacts, and accountability processes. Zero trust contributes resource-centered policy and enforcement \citep{rose2020zero}; OAL records the additional observation, lineage, timing, and consequence claims relevant to mission reliance.

\subsection{Four Complementary Assurance Claims}

Observation validity concerns correspondence with the physical world. Commitment and provenance integrity concerns protected bytes and recorded origins or transformations. Ledger and governance consistency concerns authorized shared-state transitions. Mission-use suitability concerns the particular decision and consequence. Figure~\ref{fig:assurance-chain} shows the distinct questions and supporting evidence.

A signature can remain valid after key theft; an accurate observation can become stale; and a consistent record can contain an erroneous sensor assertion. RATS appraisals support scoped measured-state claims \citep{birkholz2023remote}, while physical diagnostics and corroboration support observation claims. The relying policy composes the applicable results, with explicit responses to failed or unknown requirements.
