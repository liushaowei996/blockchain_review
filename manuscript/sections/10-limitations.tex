\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

The narrative selection and 23-study analytical sample provide an inspectable synthesis rather than exhaustive coverage. The original discovery procedure retained candidate records but not all historical rejection decisions; the revision has not reconstructed missing screening data. Fourteen original mechanism candidates remain outside full-text numerical coding, and a single extraction pass provides no independent inter-rater estimate. The heatmap is sensitive to inclusion, object/layer interpretation, and claim granularity. Empty cells and the absence of L4/L5 codes describe this sample only. Future synthesis should expand retrieval, independently duplicate coding, and report disagreement and sensitivity to alternative assignments.

OAL and CTG organize claims, dependencies, and policy interfaces. They do not supply a general inference theorem, calibrated trust model, or proof that every consequence-relevant dependency has been identified. Correlation estimates, causal interpretations, model transfer, and evidence sufficiency require application-specific validation. A scalar policy can preserve the same constraints as a structured representation, as the worked case demonstrates. Claims of improved mission outcomes therefore require the matched comparisons in Section~\ref{sec:propositions}.

The revision case uses synthetic events, a fixed coalition, protected-clock assumptions, and declared revocation semantics. It checks signatures, inclusion, deterministic policy rules, and byte lengths. It does not execute Fabric, measure consensus or acoustic performance, implement a production receipt service, or validate integrated UAV--USV--UUV safety. Full Fabric transaction sizes, provisioning overhead, cryptographic energy, clock rollback resistance, modem behavior, receipt issuance, and operator response remain implementation measurements. Communication times are serialization lower bounds; quorum sensitivities use assumed delays. These distinctions define what the reproducible illustration establishes.

Cryptographic evidence depends on protected keys, credible capture, retrievable payloads, and authenticated time or counters. An attacker who controls an unprotected key and clock can fabricate apparently historical records. A Merkle root does not recover withheld data or prove that an event was captured. Joint validation depends on genuinely independent administrative domains and an available application endorsement set. Governance deadlock, collusion beyond the fault model, and disputed physical facts require procedures outside consensus.

Transfer to maritime operations requires environmental and regulatory assessment for the actual vehicle, mission, and jurisdiction. Public datasets and component prototypes do not determine the consequences of rare faults in a deployed coalition. Field studies should evaluate privacy, retained metadata, human authority, recovery, and sustained organizational participation alongside technical behavior.
