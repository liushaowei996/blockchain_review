Dear Editors,

Please consider our Review manuscript, "Blockchain-Assisted Mission Trust in Air–Surface–Underwater Unmanned Systems: A Review and Conceptual Framework," for publication in Blockchains and inclusion in the Special Issue "Feature Papers in Blockchains 2026."

Cooperation among unmanned aerial, surface, and underwater vehicles requires mission decisions to combine evidence across heterogeneous communication links, intermittent connectivity, and organizational boundaries. This narrative review connects research on trust and reputation, provenance, remote attestation, zero trust, and permissioned blockchains through a common account of the objects being assessed, the assurance claims involved, and the mission stages at which evidence is required.

The principal contribution is the Object–Assurance–Lifecycle (OAL) framework, which relates four trust objects to four assurance layers across six lifecycle stages. A typed dependency graph and context–temporal–graph reasoning loop organize evidence dependencies, shared failure causes, uncertainty, and reliance policy. A worked example illustrates how these distinctions inform acceptance and restriction decisions. The manuscript also develops a claim-specific evidence profile, six research hypotheses, and a proposed Cross-Domain Unmanned Trust Profile to guide comparative evaluation and future interoperability work.

The review identifies conditions under which permissioned blockchains can support membership management, attestation and provenance commitments, version coordination, audit, and reconciliation after network partition. It connects these ledger functions with complementary controls for observation validity, endpoint protection, local safety, privacy, and governance. By relating blockchain architecture to explicit mission requirements and evaluation criteria, the manuscript addresses the journal's interests in blockchain applications, security, data governance, and new frameworks. Its conceptual synthesis provides a basis for future implementation and empirical evaluation.
