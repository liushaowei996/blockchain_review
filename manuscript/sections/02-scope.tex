\section{Review Scope and Methods}\label{sec:scope}

\subsection{Identification and Selection}

This is a structured narrative review. The original bibliography contained 141 references spanning unmanned cooperation, communication and security, trust inference, provenance, attestation, and distributed governance. The retained discovery script queried OpenAlex on 6 August 2026 using 32 English search strings, a publication-date filter starting on 1 January 2016, and the 25 highest-ranked results per query. Deduplication by DOI or OpenAlex identifier produced 571 candidate records. Queries included ``blockchain UAV swarm'', ``underwater wireless sensor network trust management'', ``UAV USV UUV cooperative systems'', ``remote attestation IoT survey RATS'', and ``blockchain network partition finality''. The complete query list and retrieval settings accompany the supplementary materials. Citation following and targeted retrieval added earlier foundations and official specifications. Crossref metadata supported bibliographic checking; it was not a second independent screening database.

Selection favored English sources that described cross-domain operating constraints, specified a security or trust mechanism applicable to a mission boundary, or supplied a relevant standard, dataset, or simulator. Duplicate records and sources without a substantive connection to those questions were excluded from the synthesis. Reviews supplied context and citation leads; original mechanism studies supplied the analytical sample. Foundational work predating 2016 was retained for reputation, distributed consistency, provenance, and feedback architectures. During revision, targeted searches of publisher sites, author repositories, IETF, NIST, IMO, DNV, and standards organizations added the bodies of work identified by the reviewers and checked developments through 22 October 2026.

The historical workflow did not retain a complete record of title/abstract rejection decisions. Accordingly, the 571 candidates and 141 original citations describe discovery and the original bibliography, respectively; they are not a reconstructed systematic-review screening flow. The revision supplies a disposition for every original citation and separates full-text analytical coding from contextual use. Fourteen additional original mechanism candidates remain outside the numerical map because their full-text coding was not completed. This explicit boundary permits inspection of coverage without inferring evidence from titles or abstracts.

\subsection{Full-Text Coding and Reproducible Synthesis}\label{sec:coding}

The analytical sample comprises 23 original mechanism contributions for which full texts were inspected: four UAV authentication/acquisition studies, two underwater trust studies, and 17 transferable studies of reputation, provenance, attestation, access control, shared state, or observation appraisal. These are purposively selected studies within the narrative review, rather than a census of the field. Control/planning papers, reviews, standards, and datasets remain contextual or evaluation resources. Source identifiers S01--S23, extracted claims, supporting sections/pages, deployment scope, and coding decisions are available in \texttt{oal-study-mapping.csv}. The full-text PDFs are not redistributed.

The coding unit is an explicitly described mechanism claim. Each record associates an object with an assurance layer and one or more supported lifecycle stages. Several claims can be coded for one paper; unrelated object, layer, and stage labels are never expanded into an automatic Cartesian product. The map contains 49 claim records. A heatmap cell counts distinct studies associated with that exact object--layer--stage combination; a study is counted once per cell. Counts therefore describe coverage in the coded sample and are not additive sample sizes or measures of effectiveness.

Evidence profiles record conceptual argument (L0), analytical/security reasoning (L1), simulation or recorded-data evaluation (L2), implemented prototype/testbed (L3), physical operating trials (L4), and sustained multi-party operation (L5). These categories are complementary evidence forms, not an ordinal quality score. A profile is attached to the stated mechanism and documented evaluation scope; an implementation benchmark does not validate every assurance claim made in its surrounding architecture. Recorded field data analyzed offline are coded L2; a hardware prototype without described operating conditions is coded L3. Ambiguous deployment claims are assigned the narrower evidenced category and explained in the extraction note.

Coding was prepared as a single extraction pass and checked against the cited passages and aggregation rules; independent duplicate coding and inter-rater agreement were not performed. The released CSV, codebook, and figure-generation script make the classifications inspectable and revisable. Empty cells mean that no corresponding claim was observed in this sample. They do not establish the absence of relevant work in the wider literature.
