\section{Conclusions}

Mission trust connects evidence about physical observations, protected records, jointly governed state, and fitness for a particular use. OAL organizes these claims by object and lifecycle, while a typed dependency graph and CTG loop identify when changes require reassessment. Applying the framework to 23 full-text studies makes the sampled concentrations and incomplete transitions inspectable through claim records and a coverage heatmap.

The partition--revocation case specifies coalition roles, a Fabric-oriented application pattern, constrained verification, and a reproducible communication budget. Its executable replay preserves the distinction between local authority, event-time revocation, and later audit. A policy-validating witnessed log produces the same decisions under matched rules. Ledger value is therefore conditional on shared-governance requirements and the measured cost of implementing them.

The resulting research agenda calls for independently reproducible coding, matched architecture and appraisal baselines, realistic partition and recovery tests, and integrated mission evidence. These steps can determine where the proposed organization improves decisions and accountability, and where a simpler implementation satisfies the same claims.
