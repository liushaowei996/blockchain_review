\section{Evidence Dependencies, Threat Propagation, and Dynamic Reasoning}

\subsection{A Typed Object-Dependency Graph}

Let $G_t=(V_t,E_t)$ be a time-indexed directed multigraph over OAL objects and their versions or state intervals. Relations name their semantics: an entity \emph{controls} a platform; a platform \emph{hosts} a model or \emph{produces} an observation; a path \emph{transmits} a product; an entity \emph{endorses} or \emph{consumes} a product; and a record \emph{commits to} another product. A transformation links its inputs, output, executing platform, responsible entity, code/model version, and event interval. In PROV, this transformation can be serialized as an activity \citep{w3c2013provdm}.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/figure-04-dependency-threat.pdf}
\caption{Typed dependencies and evidence states. Operational relations identify claims affected by a changed assumption. Benign degradation and adversarial intervention are causal hypotheses; unknown is an epistemic outcome.\label{fig:dependency}}
\end{figure}

Traversal identifies claims for reassessment when a key, endpoint, model, or input becomes disputed. A graph edge alone does not quantify causal influence or justify a probability update. Corroboration must account for shared sources, clocks, transformations, and administrative roots. Two products derived from one observation do not supply two independent observations. Conversely, a shared relay need not invalidate end-to-end signatures from otherwise independent sources.

\subsection{Threats, Degradation, and Missing Evidence}

An analysis statement records \emph{attacker or environmental cause; capability; affected object; propagation relation; mission consequence; control; residual uncertainty}. For example, a compromised USV can withhold UUV batches, delaying the consumer's evidence and audit; independent contacts and protected local counters may expose gaps after reconnection. Acoustic fading can create the same delivery symptom with a different cause. The response can restrict freshness-sensitive use while attribution remains unknown.

\subsection{The CTG Reasoning Loop}

CTG organizes context, temporal state, and dependencies: collect evidence; verify bindings; appraise claims and uncertainty; identify affected descendants; apply the relying policy; record the decision; and revisit it when evidence or context changes. Figure~\ref{fig:ctg} depicts this information flow.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/figure-05-ctg-reasoning-loop.pdf}
\caption{CTG appraisal and action loop, with policy, temporal state, and dependency knowledge shared across its steps.\label{fig:ctg}}
\end{figure}

The loop is positioned within established architectures. MAPE-K separates monitoring, analysis, planning, execution, and shared knowledge \citep{kephart2003autonomic,ibm2005mapek}; CTG specializes that organization for claim appraisal, event/receipt-time separation, and dependency-triggered reassessment. The JDL fusion model distinguishes object, situation, impact, and process refinement \citep{steinberg1999jdl}; CTG adds an explicit record of which identity, provenance, governance, and temporal evidence supports reliance on those estimates. Neither relationship implies a new general-purpose control or fusion algorithm.

Implementations can use Bayesian models, subjective logic, hidden-state inference, rules, or learned classifiers. Their outputs retain the named property, context, evidence lineage, uncertainty, and permitted use. Re-estimating trust after a change requires a specified update rule, rather than an arbitrary decrement along graph edges. A hard authorization condition can coexist with a graded quality estimate; the choice to make one condition non-compensable belongs to the mission policy.

\subsection{Partition, Revocation, and Reconciliation Trace}\label{sec:trace}

The coalition in Section~\ref{sec:coalition} has jointly approved policy version 1 and a low-consequence local delegation expiring at $t=480$ s. At $t=0$, the USV loses its shore connection; at $t=240$ s, shore organizations commit a revocation of a UUV signing key, effective immediately. The UUV receives the revocation at reconnection, $t=600$ s. Its protected clock reports event intervals, not exact event times. This scenario assumes a trustworthy pre-compromise time/counter record for the retained events; a stolen key alone cannot establish a credible historical timestamp.

Three signed records have intervals $[114,126]$, $[234,246]$, and $[294,306]$ s. On reconnection, a verifier checks their source signatures, batch inclusion, policy epoch, and the revocation history. A record whose interval ends before 240 s can be eligible for the historical audit. One beginning at or after 240 s is revoked at event time. An interval crossing 240 s is quarantined for review. Historical eligibility does not authorize a new physical action or refresh the observation.

The illustrative mean averages signature validity, observation quality, lineage, cached policy match, and temporal authority, using $[1,0.95,1,1,a]$ with $a=1,0.5,0$ and threshold 0.75. Its compensating aggregation accepts all three records. A scalar policy with an explicit authority veto produces exactly the typed rule's decisions. Thus this example demonstrates the effect of the chosen semantics, rather than an inherent advantage of a vector representation. Comparative inference quality remains an empirical question.

During disconnection, an action at 420 s can have restricted local authority under the cached envelope even though later audit identifies a conflict with effective global revocation; at 540 s the envelope has expired and the local policy denies the action. Reconciliation retains both the locally available authority and the later global judgment. Rewriting the old decision as if the vehicle had received the revocation at 240 s would erase this distinction.

The supplied Python replay verifies Ed25519 signatures and Merkle inclusion, rejects altered records/proofs, checks all six arrival orders with duplicate delivery, and enumerates all 16 coalition signer subsets. The ledger-policy and validating-log decision functions agree throughout. These are deterministic rule checks with synthetic events; the script runs no Fabric network or consensus protocol. Section~\ref{sec:pattern} specifies how the same application rules map to Fabric and how the constrained vehicle receives an auditable result.
