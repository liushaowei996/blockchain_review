\section{The OAL Framework and Literature Mapping}\label{sec:oal}

OAL classifies \emph{what is judged}, \emph{which claim is sought}, and \emph{when that claim is needed}. A mechanism can support several cells; some combinations are inapplicable to a mission. Dependencies between claims are represented separately so that classification does not imply evidential independence.

\subsection{Objects, Assurance Layers, and Lifecycle}

An \emph{entity} is an accountable principal: an organization, operator, service, or autonomous agent with delegated authority. A \emph{platform/device} is the endpoint executing a function, including vehicles, gateways, sensors, and hosted compute instances. A vehicle can be considered an entity when exercising delegated authority and a platform when its firmware is measured. A \emph{link/path} comprises physical media, intermediate nodes, security associations, and changing quality along an end-to-end route. A \emph{data product} is an identified version of an observation, track, map, model, command, policy, or audit record. Corrections and transformations create new version identifiers.

The assurance axis uses O (observation validity), C (commitment and provenance integrity), G (ledger and governance consistency), and M (mission-use suitability). O uses physical bounds, calibration, diagnostics, and corroboration. C binds producer, version, and transformation assertions through signatures and provenance. G addresses jointly authorized membership, order, policy, and recovery state. M applies purpose, freshness, consequence, uncertainty, and authority requirements. When a shared-state service is not needed, ledger-specific claims are inapplicable; equivalent governance claims may be met by another service.

Policies, models, and reference values are versioned data products; their deployed instances reside on platforms and their approval belongs to entities. This treatment makes the assurance infrastructure subject to the same analysis as mission observations. For each applicable cell, a specification states the object/version, required property, evidence source, validity interval, acceptance rule, responsible party, and response to missing evidence.

The map makes three integration questions concrete. First, authentication at admission requires a continuity rule before later mission use: an old credential or attestation does not specify its validity during a partition. Second, provenance and shared-record integrity during fusion require complementary observation and consequence evidence. Third, recovery must identify affected descendants and distinguish event-time authority from later receipt of revocation. No coded claim establishes an integrated air--surface--underwater mission with all three transitions validated. That is a finding about the extracted evidence scope, not an assertion that cross-domain demonstrations do not exist.

Examples show the classification's practical effect. S02 maps UAV authentication and ledger registration to admission-related claims, while its cloud-peer implementation and simulated mission geometry remain visible in the evidence note. S10 maps Hadoop/Fabric provenance to data commitment and governance at fusion and recovery, without assigning sensor-truth validation. S12 supplies platform-attestation evidence at admission; its measured TLS configurations do not establish uninterrupted device integrity until a later action. S22 contributes recorded-data observation appraisal and is not recoded as a new integrated field trial. A design review can thus identify the extra evidence needed at each handover instead of transferring an entire paper's performance claim to a different object or stage.

The UAV subset illustrates why the evidence profile accompanies the cells. Han et al. implement a Fabric-based identity service on cloud servers and use a modeled UAV setting \citep{han2022identity}. Tan et al. combine a lightweight authentication design with a Fabric implementation and simulated network geometry \citep{tan2022blockchain}. Karmakar et al. combine PUF-based authentication, clustering, and distributed ledger components \citep{karmakar2024blockchain}. BUS reports an implementation involving four IoT devices, three UAVs, a MEC server, and an Ethereum consortium \citep{islam2019bus}. Together these studies provide component precedents for admission and authenticated acquisition. The next mission-stage question is whether the accepted identity and observation remain appropriate after authority, endpoint state, or communication context changes.

The attestation subset separates architectural and implementation evidence. Hardjono and Smith analyze an attestation architecture for ledger participants, while ARCADIAN-IoT specifies a broader autonomous-security architecture \citep{hardjono2021towards,figueiredo2022arcadian}. Their coded profiles are conceptual. The implemented studies examine particular platform and protocol combinations \citep{ott2023universal,baciu2025secure}; an attested-channel result and an embedded model-integrity result support different measured-state claims. A consumer needs the reference-value version, measurement coverage, issue time, and appraisal policy before using either result. These distinctions make it possible to combine the papers without treating the word ``attestation'' as one uniform operational guarantee.

Provenance studies similarly differ in what has been exercised. The sensing platform and Hadoop integration implement record capture, integrity checks, and retrieval \citep{hang2019design,honarpajooh2021iot}; the health-IoT work implements provenance alongside federated-learning applications \citep{rahman2020secure}. Food-provenance experiments include task-specific forecasting and reported ledger evaluation \citep{khan2020iot}. Decision provenance and the digital-twin architecture describe broader chains of organizational and physical dependencies \citep{singh2019decision,suhail2022trustworthy}. OAL preserves those broader conceptual claims while keeping their evidence categories distinct from measured implementation behavior. A prototype's successful query does not demonstrate that every upstream transformation was captured.

The map also identifies a dependency between coding and design. A study concerned with node behavior can support an entity claim about cooperation, a path claim about usable forwarding, or both when the mechanism explicitly addresses them. An endpoint's measured code state is classified under platform integrity, while the accuracy of its reported location is an observation claim about a data product. These decisions are recorded in the extraction table rather than hidden in aggregate counts. Alternative reasonable assignments can be tested by editing those records and regenerating the heatmap; they should change the interpretation of coverage before they change any claim of system effectiveness.
