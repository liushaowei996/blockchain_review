\section{Introduction}

Air--surface--underwater missions combine unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), unmanned surface vehicles (USVs), and unmanned underwater vehicles (UUVs) for observation, inspection, search, and environmental monitoring. Their complementary mobility and sensing capabilities create dependencies across radio and acoustic links, gateways, processing services, and administrative owners \citep{li2023survey,wei20233u,pang2026cooperative}. UAV and marine-system reviews identify mobility, limited energy, intermittent communication, localization uncertainty, and difficult recovery as operational constraints \citep{hayat2016survey,shakhatreh2019unmanned,bae2023survey,gonzlezgarca2020autonomous}. A mission decision must combine evidence generated under these different conditions.

This creates a \emph{mission-trust gap}. Authentication establishes a principal's identity; a content hash binds bytes; replicated consensus establishes agreement under a fault model. A relying decision additionally requires evidence that the observation is physically credible, sufficiently current, and suitable for its intended consequence. A signed sonar report can be inaccurate, a genuine image can be stale, and a correctly ordered record can originate from a captured device. UAV and underwater-security research describes the corresponding spoofing, jamming, capture, routing, and data-manipulation threats \citep{mekdad2023survey,wang2025survey,hadi2023comprehensive,jiang2019securing,yisa2021security}. Connecting these controls to the eventual use of a product requires explicit claim semantics.

Blockchain proposals address swarm authentication, data acquisition, membership, provenance, and audit \citep{han2022identity,tan2022blockchain,karmakar2024blockchain,islam2019bus}. Reviews of UAV and maritime applications describe coordination and sharing opportunities \citep{hafeez2023blockchain,alladi2020applications,mehta2020blockchain,farah2024survey,li2021survey}. Their value depends on which parties must jointly govern state, which faults must be tolerated, and whether an authenticated database or independently witnessed log already satisfies those requirements. The blockchain oracle literature names the external-input problem directly: agreement about recorded assertions does not establish their correspondence with physical events \citep{caldarelli2020oracle,albreiki2020oracles}.

Adjacent mechanisms supply complementary evidence. Zero trust provides resource-specific authorization \citep{rose2020zero,syed2022zero}; remote attestation separates device evidence, appraisal, and relying decisions \citep{birkholz2023remote}; provenance records producers and transformations \citep{w3c2013provdm,w3c2013provo}; and trust inference models reputation, uncertainty, and changing behavior \citep{ahmed2019trust,aaqib2023iot,jsang2016subjective}. These mechanisms use different vocabularies and evaluate different claims. Their composition is the central subject of this review.

The proposed \OAL{} framework relates four objects---entity, platform/device, link/path, and data product---to four assurance layers---observation validity, commitment and provenance integrity, ledger and governance consistency, and mission-use suitability---across six lifecycle stages. A typed dependency graph and context--temporal--graph (CTG) loop preserve shared causes, event time, and uncertainty when applying this structure to a decision. OAL supplies the claim organization; established inference, provenance, policy, and consensus mechanisms supply implementations.

The review makes three connected contributions. First, it applies OAL to a disclosed full-text sample of 23 mechanism studies, producing a reproducible coverage map with claim locators and evidence profiles. Second, a partition--revocation--reconciliation case connects the framework to a specified permissioned-ledger pattern, an executable policy replay, and a communication budget. A policy-validating witnessed-log baseline exposes where the two architectures provide equivalent decisions. Third, requirement-based consensus selection and falsifiable research propositions translate the synthesis into an evaluation agenda. The analytical contribution concerns evidence organization and conditional technology selection; the replay evaluates specified rules rather than an integrated vehicle deployment.

The paper proceeds from review methods and operational context to the literature synthesis, OAL mapping, dependency reasoning, ledger design, evaluation practice, and research agenda. Civil missions and publicly documented coalition settings provide the application context. Limitations of coverage, implementation evidence, and transfer to operational missions are consolidated in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.
