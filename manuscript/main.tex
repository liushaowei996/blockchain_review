\Title{Blockchain-Assisted Mission Trust in Air--Surface--Underwater Unmanned Systems: A Review and Conceptual Framework}
\Author{Anonymous Author(s)}
\AuthorNames{Anonymous Author(s)}
\address{Author affiliations withheld for double-anonymous review}

\abstract{Air--surface--underwater missions combine heterogeneous vehicles, intermittent links, and administrative domains. A relying decision must connect physical observations, protected records, shared authority, and fitness for use. This structured narrative review introduces the Object--Assurance--Lifecycle (OAL) framework and applies it to 23 full-text mechanism studies through 49 claim records, a coverage heatmap, and scoped evidence profiles. The coded sample concentrates on admission integrity and data provenance/governance, while transitions to disconnected mission use require complementary evidence. A typed dependency graph and context--temporal--graph loop organize reassessment when observations, keys, policies, or dependencies change. A partition--revocation--reconciliation case specifies coalition roles, a Fabric-oriented application pattern, constrained verification, and an acoustic-link payload budget. An executable synthetic replay checks cryptographic bindings and temporal/authorization rules; a policy-validating witnessed log produces the same decisions under matched rules. Ledger value is therefore conditional on joint-governance requirements and implementation cost. Requirement-based consensus selection, falsifiable propositions, and mappings to existing assurance-case and maritime frameworks define an evaluation agenda. The contribution is a reproducible analytical synthesis and design illustration; comparative deployment performance and integrated mission effectiveness remain empirical questions.}
\keyword{mission trust; unmanned systems; UAV--USV--UUV cooperation; blockchain; data provenance; remote attestation; zero trust; maritime autonomy}
